Technology

How two hand-held units carry a question across a room, in two languages, with no network. Standard parts, a custom infrared link and firmware that fits a 32 KB processor.

A number travels

Each person reads their own language at the same moment.

The two units never send words. They send a short phrase number, and each unit shows that phrase in its own language, from pre-drawn images held in its EEPROM.

Bed Unit, set to Tamil

உங்களுக்கு வலி இருக்கிறதா?

The patient presses 1 for YES and the answer returns as another code.

  • Tiny messages. A slow, robust infrared link is fast enough.
  • Identical translation. Every phrase is checked once in advance; nothing is translated on the fly.
  • Complex scripts on a tiny screen. Sinhala and Tamil are stored as pre-drawn images, so the processor simply draws pictures.

One small, checked frame

32 bits of payload on a 2400-baud link.

Each frame is a preamble, a start byte, then destination, source and type (11 bits together), a sequence bit (1 bit), the phrase ID (12 bits) and a CRC-8 (8 bits): 32 bits of payload.

The light is a 940 nm LED on a 38 kHz carrier, received by a VS1838B. Data is 2400 baud serial, Manchester-coded for a balanced signal. The link is half duplex and each unit ignores its own transmission. The Doctor Station is address 0; Bed Units are 1 to 14.

Seven frame types

  • Question or phrase
  • Acknowledgement
  • Answer
  • Request
  • Number for an instruction
  • Link check (heartbeat and battery report)
  • Typed text (length, then two characters per frame)

A blocked beam delays, not loses

Every failure is visible and recoverable.

Infrared needs line of sight. Instead of hiding that, the protocol makes every failure visible and recoverable: the sender always knows whether the other unit received the message.

  • Acknowledge. Every frame is acknowledged; the sender shows Delivered, or NOT delivered, so the doctor can aim and resend.
  • Retry. With no acknowledgement, up to 3 retries with a random back-off, so two beds do not collide again.
  • Ignore repeats. A copy received within 8 s of the previous one is ignored, so a retry never doubles a message.
  • Listen before talking. Bed Units listen before talking, and resend an unconfirmed request or answer every 3 s (1.5 s for Can't breathe).
  • Beacon. Every 15 s the Doctor Station checks the selected bed: Link *** on the doctor, Link OK on the bed.

Urgent means unmissable.

Can't breathe repeats until someone answers.

A patient presses 7, then the request number, then #. The Doctor Station shows BED 3 REQUEST in the doctor's language. Can't breathe sounds a repeating alarm until the doctor acknowledges it, and the Bed Unit resends it every 1.5 s until confirmed.

OptiLink is an extra channel, not a safety-critical alarm. It supports, and does not replace, the ward's nurse-call system and monitors.

Written once. Drawn as images.

How Sinhala and Tamil fit on a small screen.

  1. 1. Every phrase is written once in a table: English, Sinhala and Tamil, with its ID and answer type.
  2. 2. A Python tool renders Sinhala and Tamil with proper text shaping (Pillow with libraqm, Noto fonts).
  3. 3. Each rendering becomes a run-length compressed 1-bit image.
  4. 4. Images and an index load into each unit's EEPROM (content version 3).
  5. 5. The firmware draws English with its own font and Sinhala or Tamil from images.

What the units hold

26 entries in four groups (General, Pain, Symptoms, Care & meds): 21 questions and 5 instructions with a number.

Answer types: yes/no, acknowledge, choose one, choose several (body parts), pain 0-10, a number.

Assessments (New patient, Breathing, Fever, Stomach, plus the pain sequence) each end in one English sentence, for example "Patient has pain in the chest, severity 6/10, for 1-3 days, constant."

Typed messages are English, up to 48 characters, both directions, with 6 ready phrases.

The patient report, kept when the unit is switched off, holds 24 entries and lists every answer and request as an English sentence, grouped by bed.

One station, fourteen beds

Identical hardware, roles set by firmware.

One Doctor Station addresses up to 14 numbered Bed Units. It talks to one selected bed at a time but receives from every bed.

The firmware, not the hardware, decides the role, so spares are interchangeable.

Standard parts, one board

An ATmega328P with a screen, keypad, phrase memory and infrared link.

ControllerATmega328P-PU, 16 MHz, Uno bootloader, socketed
Power9 V PP3 NiMH, rocker switch, 1N5819, MP1584 buck at 5.0 V
IR transmitter74HC04 + 74HC08, S8050, 940 nm LED, 33 ohm 1/2 W
IR receiverVS1838B 38 kHz with RC supply filter
Display1.3" SH1106 OLED, 128 x 64, I2C
Phrase memoryAT24C256 EEPROM, 32 KB
Input and alert4 x 4 membrane keypad; 5 V buzzer on a second S8050

Transmitter, step by step

  1. Timer2 makes a continuous 38 kHz square wave.
  2. The serial TX line passes through a 74HC04 inverter and a 74HC08 AND gate with the carrier.
  3. An S8050 drives the 940 nm LED: about 100 mA pulses.
  4. A pull-up keeps the LED off if the processor is removed.
  5. The VS1838B removes the carrier and returns clean serial data.

Like a TV remote, the LED flashes at 38 kHz while a data bit is low, and stays dark otherwise.

Warns before it fails

Two thresholds, one report to the doctor.

Each unit runs on a 9 V PP3 NiMH rechargeable battery, through a 1N5819 diode into an MP1584 buck converter at 5.0 V. A 100k / 47k divider feeds the processor.

Battery voltageStateIndication
Above 7.0 VNormalNo warning
6.4 to 7.0 VLOWRed light blinks, one beep, a warning
Below 6.4 VCRITICALLight on, three beeps; a Bed Unit also alerts the Doctor Station

A 0.2 V hysteresis stops flickering between levels. The screen sleeps after inactivity to save power.

Everything inside 32 KB

Phrase images live in a separate memory chip.

Doctor Station program: 32,128 of 32,256 bytes

75.8%

Phrase images in EEPROM: 29,219 of 32,768 bytes (content version 3)

Every stage stayed inside the ATmega328P's 32 KB. That discipline is why the phrase images moved to the EEPROM, and why the next step is a larger processor.

Stages

  • 1 Assessments, numbers in phrases, patient requests.
  • 2 English report, multi-select body parts, new patient.
  • 3 Typed English messages both ways.
  • 4 Several beds, link indicator, urgent alarm, battery monitor, screen sleep.
  • 4.4 Link improvements, one-second hold to go home.
  • 4.6 Bed Unit on digits 0-9, * and # alone.

Why 4.6: the A-D column of the membrane keypad proved unreliable.
